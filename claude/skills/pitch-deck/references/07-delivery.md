# Delivery

Saying it out loud: pace, interruptions, knowing your own numbers, and whether to animate.

## Delivery

Andreessen Horowitz recommends preparing a full deck, script, and dry runs rather than relying on an improvised conversation.

Source:

- https://a16z.com/16-common-questions-about-fundraising/

First Round describes a pitch as something that should be rehearsed enough to remain natural while being interruptible.

Source:

- https://review.firstround.com/the-fundraising-wisdom-that-helped-our-founders-raise-18b-in-follow-on-capital/

### Practical delivery principles

- Speak to the investor, not the screen.
- Pause after important claims.
- Use the slide as evidence.
- Keep answers concise.
- Admit what you do not know.
- Return to the narrative after questions.
- Maintain energy without sounding theatrical.

### Never read your slides aloud

This is the one delivery rule with a mechanism behind it. Text on the slide and speech compete for one channel, so neither lands. See ../SKILL.md for the rule as stated in the core set.

The practical consequence: the slide carries the claim, and you say the part that is not written down. If your script is the slide text, cut the slide text.

## Practice for Interruptions

Investor meetings are not conference keynotes.

First Round warns that partner meetings can involve frequent out-of-order questions and recommends rehearsing with deliberate interruptions.

Source:

- https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/

### Rehearsal exercise

Ask a colleague to:

- Interrupt on slide 2 with a slide-12 question
- Ask about pricing during market
- Ask about competition during product
- Challenge a key assumption
- Ask you to jump forward
- Ask for a specific metric

Your goal is to answer and return to the narrative without sounding scripted.

## Know Your Numbers

Partner meetings frequently involve investors who have reviewed internal materials and are prepared to ask detailed questions.

First Round recommends founders know their metrics extremely well.

Source:

- https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/

The standard is recall, not retrieval. You should be able to answer without searching for any of it.

The metrics that belong on the Traction, Business Model and Financials slides are listed in 03-slide-by-slide.md. They are revenue and growth, gross margin, retention and cohort behavior, pipeline, conversion, ACV/AOV and customer count. Know all of those to the same standard.

Beyond that list, expect these and have them ready:

- Pricing
- Fundraise history
- Ownership basics
- Burn, cash and runway

## Animation and Transitions

Harvard recommends using animations and special effects sparingly.

Sources:

- https://catalyst.harvard.edu/writing-communication-center/visualize-science/slides/
- https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/health-communication/resources/slide-checklist/

### Good uses

- Reveal a process step-by-step
- Build a complex diagram progressively
- Compare before/after
- Reveal annotations
- Walk through product interaction

### Weak uses

- Text flying in
- Rotating objects
- Decorative motion
- Different transitions on every slide

### The closing rule

Meaning must never depend on an animation. A PDF export loses every build, every reveal and every transition. A slide whose point only appears after the third click has no point at all in the sent version. Design the slide to read as a still frame, then add motion on top if it helps you narrate it.
