# Which deck, how long

A projected deck and an emailed deck are different products. Slide count, length and stage follow from which one you are building.

## Build a Deck System, Not One Universal Deck

One of the biggest improvements to pitch-deck practice is to stop treating “the deck” as a single artifact.

Techstars’ current fundraising-material guidance distinguishes several materials for different moments in the process.

Source:

- https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/techstars-fundraising-series-prepping-your-materials

### Recommended deck ecosystem

#### A. Forwardable / emailable deck

Purpose:

- Get the meeting.
- Be understandable without the founder present.
- Travel internally inside an investment firm.
- Make the company easy to summarize to another partner.

Characteristics:

- Concise.
- Self-contained.
- Slightly more explanatory text than a live deck.
- Strong company-purpose slide.
- Clear proof points.
- Minimal confidential detail.
- No animations required for comprehension.

Techstars’ toolkit notes that founders commonly use **PDF or controlled document-sharing links** for emailable decks.

Source:

- https://toolkit.techstars.com/build-your-investor-pipeline

#### B. Meeting deck

Purpose:

- Guide a discussion.
- Provide visual anchors.
- Allow the founder to control sequencing without turning the meeting into a lecture.

Characteristics:

- Larger type.
- More visual.
- Less body text.
- Designed to survive interruptions.
- Strong navigation and section logic.

Techstars describes the investor meeting deck as a narrative presentation and advises founders to expect interruptions.

Source:

- https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/techstars-fundraising-series-prepping-your-materials

#### C. Drill-down / appendix deck

Purpose:

- Answer detailed investor questions immediately.
- Demonstrate command of metrics and operating details.
- Support partner meetings and diligence.

Possible appendix slides:

- Cohort retention
- Revenue concentration
- Sales pipeline
- CAC detail
- LTV assumptions
- Gross margin evolution
- Product roadmap
- Hiring plan
- Competitive landscape
- Market calculation
- Funnel conversion
- Customer segmentation
- Pricing sensitivity
- Financial assumptions
- Security/regulatory detail where relevant

First Round specifically recommends having a large appendix available in partner meetings and gives examples such as cohort analysis, competitive landscape, customer revenue breakdown, and sales pipeline.

Source:

- https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/

#### D. Data room

The deck is not the data room.

Andreessen Horowitz describes the pitch deck as only one part of diligence alongside items such as cap table, historical P&L, burn, and other supporting documents.

Source:

- https://a16z.com/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/

## Stage-Specific Differences

### Pre-seed

At pre-seed, the deck often needs to prove:

- Founder insight
- Problem importance
- Why now
- Early user/customer discovery
- Product insight/prototype
- Market potential
- Team-market fit
- Credible first milestones

DocSend’s updated **2026-02-24** pre-seed research reports investors spending approximately **4 minutes 10 seconds** reviewing a pre-seed deck on average.

Source:

- https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-to-include-when-building-your-pre-seed-pitch-deck/

#### Pre-seed emphasis

More:

- Insight
- Founder credibility
- Product vision
- Customer evidence
- Why now

Less:

- Long financial histories
- Mature unit economics
- Over-engineered five-year forecasts

### Seed

At seed, stronger evidence is normally expected:

- Repeatable usage
- Early revenue or strong demand signals
- Growth
- Retention
- Distribution learning
- Business-model clarity
- Capital efficiency

DocSend’s 2026 seed research emphasizes capital efficiency, realistic projections, a path toward sustainable economics, and evidence-backed claims.

Source:

- https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/

### Series A

At Series A, the deck should usually demonstrate:

- Product-market fit
- Meaningful scale
- Growth consistency
- Cohort/retention evidence
- A scalable GTM motion
- Unit economics
- Hiring ability
- Defensibility
- A credible route to a very large outcome

NFX’s Series A framework emphasizes traction, PMF, scalability, unit economics, vision, defensibility, and a world-class presentation of data.

Source:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/fundraising-checklist-13-proof-points-series-a

## Slide Count

There is no universal correct number.

Different credible sources recommend different constraints because they describe different artifacts.

Slide count is a property of the artifact, not of the company. The same company can be right at ten live slides and right again at twenty sent pages, because the two products do different work. For which artifacts exist and what each one is for, see "Build a Deck System, Not One Universal Deck" above.

### What the sources say

#### DocSend seed research

Approximately **19-20 pages** for a full seed deck. That figure is the DocSend dataset number. Do not widen it into a rounder band unless you label the wider band a judgment of your own.

Source:

- https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/

#### Guy Kawasaki

Ten slides, as the slide-count half of the 10/20/30 heuristic covered below.

Source:

- https://guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch/

#### Techstars

Current fundraising-material guidance describes a meeting deck intended to tell the story in a short presentation and expects interruptions.

Source:

- https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/techstars-fundraising-series-prepping-your-materials

### Guy Kawasaki 10/20/30

Guy Kawasaki’s famous heuristic is:

- 10 slides
- 20 minutes
- 30-point minimum type

Source:

- https://guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch/

It is useful as a discipline for live pitches, especially when a deck is becoming bloated.

It is a heuristic, and it must not override:

- DocSend’s longer read-alone seed decks.
- First Round’s partner-meeting appendix guidance.
- Techstars’ multi-deck system.
- The type scale in `04-type-and-layout.md`. Kawasaki’s 30 pt is a floor for live body copy, not a license to set headlines at 30 pt. Take headline and body sizes from the two tables in that file.

## Live Pitch Length

500 Global recommends keeping initial investor presentations under roughly 20 minutes and leaving substantial time for discussion.

Source:

- https://500.co/content/top-19-insights-on-startup-fundraising-in-a-remote-environment

Techstars’ current materials guidance describes a shorter meeting narrative and emphasizes expecting interruptions.

Source:

- https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/techstars-fundraising-series-prepping-your-materials

### Slides per minute

Harvard gives a general live-presentation heuristic of approximately one slide per minute.

Source:

- https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/health-communication/resources/slide-checklist/

That is a general presentation heuristic and it does not apply to an investor pitch. An investor meeting is interrupted, the running order bends to the questions asked, and a single slide can hold the screen for several minutes of discussion. Use it to sanity-check a lecture or a conference talk, not to size a pitch deck.

### Practical default

For a scheduled 30-minute meeting:

- 8-12 minutes prepared core story
- Remaining time discussion

For a 60-minute partner meeting:

- Prepare a core version and a longer version
- Expect interruptions
- Keep appendix ready

First Round specifically recommends practicing both short and long versions.

Source:

- https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/

### Enforced (see SKILL.md)

The speaker notes are the script, so they are checkable against the clock. Pass the length you actually have, for example the 10 minutes at the middle of the prepared core story above:

```bash
python3 scripts/audit_deck.py deck.pptx --minutes 10
```

Notes word count divided by 110 words a minute. If the notes do not fit, the
pitch does not fit, and a moderator with a hard stop will cut you off mid
sentence. Aim at about 85% of the limit so there is room to breathe, and note
that spelled-out numbers cost real speaking time.
