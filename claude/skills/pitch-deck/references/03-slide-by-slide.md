# Slide by slide

What belongs on each slide, what a strong version looks like, and what to avoid.

## Slide-by-Slide Recommendations

### 9.1 Cover / company-purpose slide

#### Must answer

> What do you do?

#### Recommended content

- Logo/name
- One-sentence description
- Optional product visual or customer outcome
- Optional key proof point if genuinely remarkable

#### Better one-liner structure

> **[Company] helps [specific customer] achieve [valuable outcome] by [distinct mechanism].**

Alternative:

> **We automate [specific task] for [specific customer], reducing [pain/cost/time] by [measurable result].**

Sequoia recommends defining the company in a single declarative sentence.

Source:

- https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/

NFX also has extensive guidance around capturing the essence of a business in one sentence.

Source:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/the-nfx-pitch-deck-library

#### Avoid

- “AI-powered next-generation platform”
- “Revolutionizing the future of X”
- Multiple unexplained category terms
- Mission statements that do not explain the product

---

### 9.2 Problem

#### Must answer

> Is this a painful, frequent, expensive, risky, or strategically valuable problem?

Strong evidence includes:

- Customer behavior
- Current workflow
- Existing spend
- Time wasted
- Revenue lost
- Regulatory risk
- Failure rates
- Customer interviews
- Customer quotes summarized rather than overused
- Existing workaround costs

Sequoia recommends describing the customer pain and current solutions/shortcomings.

Source:

- https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/

#### Better headline

Weak:

> **Problem**

Strong:

> **Finance teams still spend 14 hours each week reconciling fragmented data**

The second headline gives the investor a claim to evaluate.

---

### 9.3 Solution

#### Must answer

> What changed for the customer?

Show:

- Before vs after
- Workflow reduction
- Speed improvement
- Cost reduction
- Quality increase
- New capability
- Risk reduction

Do not turn the solution slide into a feature matrix.

The best solution slide normally explains the outcome first and the mechanism second.

---

### 9.4 Why now

Sequoia explicitly includes “Why now?” in its framework.

Source:

- https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/

NFX also emphasizes timing as central to startup narratives.

Sources:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/the-nfx-pitch-deck-library
- https://www.nfx.com/post/fundraising-checklist-13-proof-points-series-a

A strong “why now” can come from:

- New regulation
- Cost collapse
- Platform shift
- AI/model capability
- Hardware availability
- Consumer behavior change
- Distribution change
- New data availability
- Demographic shift
- Market consolidation
- Legacy technology reaching a breaking point

#### Avoid

Generic trend charts that are only loosely connected to the startup.

---

### 9.5 Market

#### Strong method

Use a bottom-up market model where possible:

> Number of realistic customers × annual spend per customer = addressable opportunity

NFX recommends bottom-up TAM thinking.

Source:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/the-non-obvious-guide-to-fundraising

DocSend’s updated seed guidance also warns against inflated market claims and recommends clearly identifying the initial customer segment.

Source:

- https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/

#### Useful market layers

- **Beachhead**: first segment you can win
- **SAM**: practical serviceable market
- **TAM**: long-run total potential
- **Expansion**: adjacent segments/products

#### Avoid

> “The global software market is $800B. If we capture 1%, we are an $8B business.”

This shows arithmetic, not a go-to-market thesis.

---

### 9.6 Product

YC recommends graphics, charts, and screenshots over large quantities of words.

Source:

- https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/

DocSend’s 2026 guidance recommends showing the product clearly rather than describing it abstractly.

Source:

- https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/

#### Good product slide

- One screenshot
- One highlighted workflow
- One sentence explaining the user value
- One proof point

#### Better than

- Full-screen UI screenshot
- 12 tiny annotations
- 8 feature cards
- Unreadable navigation chrome

---

### 9.7 Traction

Useful evidence can include:

- Revenue
- ARR/MRR
- Revenue growth
- Gross margin
- Active users
- Paid users
- Retention
- Cohort behavior
- Engagement
- Usage frequency
- Conversion
- Pipeline
- Pilots
- Signed contracts
- LOIs
- Waitlist
- Product usage
- Expansion revenue
- Renewal
- Customer references

#### Principle

Show trend, scale and quality.

A number without context is weak.

For example:

Weak:

> 1,000 customers

Stronger:

> 1,000 paid customers, up from 240 twelve months ago, with 92% annual retention

NFX’s Series A guidance emphasizes consistent month-to-month growth, retention/PMF evidence, and scale.

Source:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/fundraising-checklist-13-proof-points-series-a

#### Avoid vanity metrics

Examples:

- Page views without conversion
- Waitlist size without activation
- Downloads without retention
- Social impressions
- Cumulative signups when active usage is weak

---

### 9.8 Go-to-market / distribution

This slide answers:

> How does the company acquire customers repeatedly?

Include:

- Initial ICP
- Channel
- Sales cycle
- Conversion
- Acquisition motion
- ACV/AOV
- CAC where meaningful
- Payback where meaningful
- Expansion motion
- Partnerships where important

NFX explicitly includes distribution in its recommended pitch-deck elements.

Source:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/the-non-obvious-guide-to-fundraising

---

### 9.9 Business model

DocSend’s 2026 seed guidance reports particularly high investor attention on business-model content and emphasizes clear paths toward sustainable economics.

Source:

- https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/

Answer:

- Who pays?
- What do they buy?
- How much do they pay?
- How frequently?
- What drives expansion?
- What gross margin is possible?
- What are the cost drivers?
- What happens at scale?

#### Good visual formats

- Pricing ladder
- Unit-economics bridge
- Customer economics
- Revenue equation
- Gross-margin evolution
- Simple funnel

#### Avoid

A screenshot of a spreadsheet.

---

### 9.10 Competition / alternatives

Sequoia recommends discussing direct and indirect competition.

Source:

- https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/

Competition includes:

- Direct startups
- Incumbents
- Internal/manual workflows
- Consultants
- Spreadsheets
- “Do nothing”
- Adjacent software

#### Strong competitive slides

- Comparison based on criteria customers care about
- Market map with clear axes
- Workflow comparison
- Cost/time/outcome comparison
- Positioning based on defensible strengths

#### Weak competitive slides

- Your logo alone in the upper-right corner of a 2×2
- Competitors intentionally placed on irrelevant axes
- “No competition”
- A wall of logos with no insight

---

### 9.11 Defensibility

Potential sources of defensibility:

- Network effects
- Proprietary data
- Distribution advantage
- Scale economies
- Workflow embedding
- Switching costs
- Regulatory position
- Brand
- Unique supply
- Technical advantage
- Learning loops

NFX stresses defensibility strongly in Series A fundraising.

Source:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/fundraising-checklist-13-proof-points-series-a

Avoid using “moat” as a label without showing how the advantage compounds.

---

### 9.12 Team

#### Answer

> Why are these people unusually likely to solve this problem?

Show relevance, not full biographies.

Useful proof:

- Domain insight
- Prior company-building
- Technical credibility
- Customer access
- Unique research
- Distribution expertise
- Prior collaboration
- Relevant outcomes
- Exceptional recruiting

NFX emphasizes the ability to attract talent as a positive signal at later stages.

Source:

- https://www.nfx.com/post/fundraising-checklist-13-proof-points-series-a

#### Better format

**Name, role.** One sentence explaining the relevant unfair advantage.

#### Avoid

- Full LinkedIn histories
- University logos with no relevance
- 12 advisors
- Tiny bios
- Vanity credentials without a connection to execution

---

### 9.13 Financials

At early stage, the financial slide should explain the economic model, not pretend the future is precisely predictable.

Useful elements:

- Historical revenue
- Burn
- Cash
- Runway
- Gross margin
- Hiring assumptions
- Major operating drivers
- Scenario range

Andreessen Horowitz recommends historical P&L and burn as core diligence information.

Source:

- https://a16z.com/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/

DocSend’s 2026 guidance favors realistic projections over implausible hockey-stick forecasts.

Source:

- https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/

### 9.14 Fundraising ask

The ask is a milestone story. The single best framing question is:

> Where does this round take the company?

To answer it, the slide should cover:

- How much are you raising?
- What runway does it provide?
- What milestones will it achieve?
- What will be true at the next financing that is not true today?

Techstars’ 2026-05-07 guidance recommends framing the raise around the milestones the money enables, not simply percentage allocation to departments.

Source:

- https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/why-most-pitch-decks-dont-work-and-how-to-make-sure-yours-does

#### Strong ask

> Raising $3M to reach $4M ARR, launch the enterprise product, and prove <12-month CAC payback

#### Weaker ask

> 50% engineering / 30% sales / 20% operations

#### Worked contrast

Instead of:

> We are raising $3M for 50% engineering, 30% GTM, 20% operations.

Use:

> We are raising $3M to reach $4M ARR, establish repeatable enterprise sales, ship the compliance platform, and reach Series A with 24 months of operating capacity.

The second version names runway, repeatability, and the state of the company at the next financing.

The departmental percentages can live in the appendix.

---

### 9.15 Vision

Sequoia recommends articulating what the company can become over the long term.

Source:

- https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/

The final strategic slide should answer:

> If this succeeds, what important company exists in five or ten years?

The vision should be larger than the current feature set but logically connected to the initial wedge.
