# Sources and rule strength

Which rules are hard and which are flexible, and the annotated source hierarchy behind them. One bibliography: each entry carries its tier, what it is good for, its links, and a publication date where one exists.

## Evidence Strength: Which Rules Are Hard and Which Are Flexible?

| Recommendation | Strength | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 16:9 widescreen | Strong default, named exceptions | Official PowerPoint default and recommended presentation format; a different canvas is allowed when the venue or the print format calls for it |
| One main idea per slide | Strong | YC, MIT, Stanford converge |
| Assertion/takeaway headlines | Strong | MIT, Harvard, startup practice converge |
| 18 pt text minimum, live deck | Strong accessibility floor for a projected deck | Harvard/Microsoft-style accessibility guidance. The separate 14 pt floor for a read-alone deck is a judgment call made for this skill and is not attributable to Harvard |
| Live body text at 24 pt and up | Strong practical default | Stanford and presentation-distance considerations. The full canonical scale, live and read-alone, lives in the Typography section of `04-type-and-layout.md`; use that, not a number restated elsewhere |
| 4.5:1 contrast | Strong accessibility target | WCAG |
| Use color sparingly for emphasis | Strong | Tableau/Datawrapper/accessibility sources |
| At most three hue families per deck | Judgment call for this skill | The direction, fewer colors, is Datawrapper and Tableau; the cap of three is ours. Tints of one hue count as one family. Semantic colors (positive, negative, warning) are a named exception, not a fourth family |
| At most six objects per slide | Single-source | Phillips only, and from a talk transcript. The six-object figure is a working-memory claim made in the talk, not a cited experiment |
| Minimum 1.8 times size contrast between headline and body | Judgment call for this skill | The direction, size signals importance, is Phillips; the 1.8 ratio itself is ours, chosen so hierarchy survives projection |
| Speaker notes fit the clock | Judgment call for this skill | Follows from the 8 to 12 minute live core story, but the check itself is ours, not a sourced rule |
| Direct-label charts | Strong where possible | Datawrapper |
| Source line kept on the slide | Practical default | Keep it at the mode floor, 18 pt live and 14 pt read-alone, and move only overflow provenance to the appendix. Dropping the on-slide line is acceptable for a live projected deck, where the presenter says the provenance out loud |
| 10 slides | Heuristic | Kawasaki. A useful live-pitch constraint, not universal, and it does not override the type scale, the DocSend read-alone lengths, the appendix guidance or the multi-deck system |
| 19 to 20 pages seed deck | Evidence-backed context | DocSend dataset; not a universal law. Do not widen the band without labeling the wider number a judgment |
| One slide per minute | General heuristic | Useful for talks, not investor conversations |
| Light background | Context-dependent | Strong safe default, not mandatory |
| One font family | Practical default | Consistency and readability. Two families is the ceiling; an optional monospace for code or technical identifiers does not spend the second slot |
| 5 to 7 memorable ideas | Strong heuristic | YC memory/clarity guidance |
| PDF for sending | Strong practical default | Formatting stability; Techstars/Microsoft |
| Large appendix | Strong for partner meetings | First Round |
| Milestone-based ask | Strong current investor advice | Techstars 2026 |

## Source Hierarchy

Three tiers, strongest first. Every source used anywhere in this reference set appears once, here.

### Tier 1: Investor behavior and fundraising guidance

The most important sources for pitch-deck **content and process**.

#### Dropbox DocSend

Tier 1. Engagement data drawn from real seed and pre-seed decks: section ordering, where investor attention actually goes, and page counts. This is the source behind the 19 to 20 page read-alone seed deck figure.

- What VCs Really Want to See Inside Your Seed Deck, updated 2026-03-10: https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/
- What to Include When Building Your Pre-Seed Pitch Deck, updated 2026-02-24: https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-to-include-when-building-your-pre-seed-pitch-deck/
- Data-Driven Answers to Frequently Asked Questions on Startup Fundraising: https://www.docsend.com/blog/data-driven-answers-to-frequently-asked-questions-on-startup-fundraising/

Caveat: DocSend is also a document-sharing vendor, so lean on its engagement datasets more heavily than on its product-specific sharing claims.

#### Sequoia Capital

Tier 1. Investor narrative structure: company purpose, problem, solution, why now, market, competition, business model, team, financials, vision.

- Writing a Business Plan: https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/

#### Y Combinator

Tier 1. Seed fundraising mechanics and deck structure: graphics over text, simple design, few memorable points.

- A Guide to Seed Fundraising: https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/
- How to Design a Better Pitch Deck: https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck

#### Techstars

Tier 1. Current 2026 pitch storytelling, deck types, meeting behavior, milestone framing, and what to prepare.

- Why Most Pitch Decks Do Not Work and How to Make Sure Yours Does, 2026-05-07: https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/why-most-pitch-decks-dont-work-and-how-to-make-sure-yours-does
- Fundraising Series: Prepping Your Materials: https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/techstars-fundraising-series-prepping-your-materials
- Building Your Investor Pipeline: https://toolkit.techstars.com/build-your-investor-pipeline

#### First Round

Tier 1. Partner meeting dynamics: appendix preparation, rehearsing interruptions, short and long versions, narrative and process.

- What to Expect in a Seed-Stage VC Partner Meeting: https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/
- Fundraising Wisdom and Pitch Assist: https://review.firstround.com/the-fundraising-wisdom-that-helped-our-founders-raise-18b-in-follow-on-capital/

#### NFX

Tier 1. Investor mental models: bottom-up market sizing, distribution, defensibility, Series A proof points, plus a library of real decks.

- The NFX Pitch Deck Library: https://www.nfx.com/post/the-nfx-pitch-deck-library
- The Non-Obvious Guide to Fundraising: https://www.nfx.com/post/the-non-obvious-guide-to-fundraising
- Fundraising Checklist: 13 Proof Points for Series A: https://www.nfx.com/post/fundraising-checklist-13-proof-points-series-a

#### Andreessen Horowitz

Tier 1. Fundraising preparation, diligence, data-room expectations, and the case for practicing the pitch.

- 16 Common Questions About Fundraising: https://a16z.com/16-common-questions-about-fundraising/
- The Insider's Guide to Data Rooms: What to Know Before You Raise: https://a16z.com/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/

#### 500 Global

Tier 1. Live fundraising meetings: how long you actually present, and why the meeting is a discussion rather than a recital.

- Top 19 Insights on Startup Fundraising in a Remote Environment: https://500.co/content/top-19-insights-on-startup-fundraising-in-a-remote-environment
- Pitch Please: 14 Must-Read Pitch Lessons Every Startup Founder Should Know: https://500.co/content/pitch-please-14-must-read-pitch-lessons-every-startup-founder-should-know

#### Guy Kawasaki

Tier 1, but a heuristic rather than a dataset. The 10/20/30 rule: ten slides, twenty minutes, thirty point type. Useful as a live-pitch discipline. It does not override the type scale in `04-type-and-layout.md`, the DocSend read-alone page counts, the appendix guidance, or the multi-deck system.

- The Only 10 Slides You Need in Your Pitch: https://guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch/

### Tier 2: Presentation design and accessibility

#### Microsoft PowerPoint

Tier 2. Official product behavior: slide sizes, font embedding, accessibility checks, and PDF export.

- Change the Size of Your PowerPoint Slides: https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/PowerPoint/change-the-size-of-your-powerpoint-slides
- Benefits of Embedding Custom Fonts: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/benefits-of-embedding-custom-fonts-cb3982aa-ea76-4323-b008-86670f222dbc
- Make Your PowerPoint Presentations Accessible to People With Disabilities: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/powerpoint/make-your-powerpoint-presentations-accessible-to-people-with-disabilities
- Save PowerPoint Presentations as PDF Files: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/training/save-powerpoint-presentations-as-pdf-files

#### MIT Communication Lab

Tier 2. One idea per slide, assertion titles, and choosing a figure based on the takeaway you want.

- Slideshow: https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/slideshow/
- Figure Design: https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/figure-design/

#### Harvard

Tier 2. Headline-driven slide design, white space, font-size recommendations, accessibility, and high-resolution visuals. Harvard is the source for the 18 pt accessibility floor on a projected deck. It is not the source for the 14 pt read-alone floor, which is a judgment call made for this skill.

- Harvard T.H. Chan, Slide Design Checklist: https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/health-communication/resources/slide-checklist/
- Harvard Catalyst, Slides: https://catalyst.harvard.edu/writing-communication-center/visualize-science/slides/
- Harvard Digital Accessibility, PowerPoint: https://accessibility.huit.harvard.edu/microsoft-powerpoint

#### Stanford

Tier 2. Large fonts, one idea per slide, visual rather than text-heavy slides, simple design.

- Oral Communication Program, Visuals and PowerPoint: https://oralcommprogram.stanford.edu/presentation-and-delivery/visuals-and-powerpoint

#### W3C

Tier 2. Contrast ratios, use of color, and the underlying accessibility principles.

- WCAG 2.2: https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
- Understanding Use of Color: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color

#### David JP Phillips

Tier 2. "How to Avoid Death by PowerPoint", TEDxStockholmSalon. This is the sole source for three rules the audit enforces: the six-object working-memory budget per slide, size signals importance, and contrast directs the eye.

- Talk transcript: https://singjupost.com/how-to-avoid-death-by-powerpoint-david-jp-phillips-at-tedxstockholmsalon-transcript/

Caveat: this is a talk transcript, so it is a weaker class of evidence than the institutional sources beside it. The six-object figure in particular is a working-memory claim made in the talk, not a cited experiment. Treat the direction as sound and the exact number as a working budget.
